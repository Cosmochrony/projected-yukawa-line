  \begin{abstract}
    This note opens the mass-sector frontier of the fermionic-matter sub-programme of Cosmochrony.
    It derives no mass, Yukawa coupling, or mixing.
    It records two statements, each with the hypothesis it needs.
    First, algebra proved on supplied model data: on a rank-two bundle with structure group in
    $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, the determinant line $\wedge^{2}$ is the unique one-dimensional functor among the symmetric and
    exterior powers, and it is trivial.
    Hence $\wedge^{2}(\SW)$ carries no hypercharge under the spin solder [H-Spin] of Q14.
    Under the distinct weak factor [H-Weak] of Q14, which is not supplied and not constructed here, the line
    $\LY := \wedge^{2}(\Ew)$ with structure group $\UU(2)$ carries the abelian weight: within this functor route it is
    a conditional weak determinant line, not a line of the spinor bundle.
    Second, on the generation triplet $\Cgen$, the model operator $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ has exit
    deficits $\{0, \tfrac12 - u, \tfrac12 + u\}$ that order three levels for $0 < u < \tfrac12$, with a
    scale-independent ratio.
    Reading it as the restriction of the squared projective residue $\Epi^{2}$ is a named hypothesis, [H-Res], not
    supplied, and the map from levels to generations is not established.
    The Yukawa operator, which needs a weak linking carrier supplied by no source, its norm, the sign of $u$, and the
    mixing remain open, so no mass value is claimed.
    Interpretive outlook (a reading, not a result): where the Yukawa coupling attaches to hypercharge becomes the
    question of where a distinct weak factor comes from, and the levels are a dimensionless pattern whose reading as a
    hierarchy awaits the missing identifications.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Position and purpose}
    \label{sec:position}

    The fermionic-matter sub-programme asks which electroweak and generation structure the admissible spinor bundle can
    carry.
    The constituent paper~\cite{Beau2026q14} types the readings separately.
    On supplied model data the tensor algebra is proved: $\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2}) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$
    is the adjoint and $\wedge^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})$ is the determinant line.
    The Lorentz reading is conditional on the spin solder [H-Spin]: $\Sym^{2}(\SW)$ is the Lorentz sector and
    $\wedge^{2}(\SW)$ is a trivial line.
    The electroweak reading is conditional on a distinct rank-two weak factor [H-Weak] with structure group $\UU(2)$:
    $\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}$ sits in $\Sym^{2}(\Ew)$ and $\LY := \wedge^{2}(\Ew)$ carries $\UU(1)_{Y}$.
    Neither hypothesis is supplied by a source, and $\Ew$ is not constructed.
    The $V-A$ structure is the representation content of [H-Weak] and is not derived from the left-admissibility of the
    projected Dirac residue $\Epi$.
    Left-admissibility is an input of Q14 (Proposition~3.8 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}): it is a statement about the Lorentz
    chirality on the orientation-compatible branch, conditional on [H-Spin] and on the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of
    O30~\cite{Beau2026a34}.
    The generation factor $\Cgen$ is a gauge-singlet multiplicity space, conditional on the supplied rank-three
    selection
    rule, and is the symmetric square of a distinct supplied doublet, not of the soldered spinor doublet.
    Q14 describes the transfer of the identification of O30 to this triplet as a further modelling step.
    The split coefficient of $\Cgen$ is a single real number $u$.
    The projective-residue Schur analysis~\cite{Beau2026prs} states the squared residue on the supplied triplet in the
    normal form of Section~\ref{sec:levels} and reduces the question $u \neq 0$ to a chiral defect.
    This note reads that normal form as $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ only under the named identification [H-Res].
    The Born--Infeld margin note~\cite{Beau2026bim} analyses the radial factor of the modulus of $u$ and does not
    predict $|u|$.
    The sub-programme note states plainly that the mass spectrum and the Yukawa couplings are not derived, and lists
    them among the open deliverables~\cite{Beau2026fmnote}.

    This note takes up the first stage of that open deliverable, and only the first stage.
    It does not compute a mass.
    It records two structural statements, the weak determinant line and the generation-level ordering, each with its
    hypothesis, and it isolates, as the genuinely open downstream object, the projected Yukawa operator whose norm and
    mixing carry the absolute mass scale and the flavour structure.
    The method is stated once and held throughout: under [H-Weak] the determinant line $\LY$ supplies the line from
    which the weights of the coupling are built, the exponents $k$ and $m$ not being fixed by [H-Weak] alone; the
    model operator on $\Cgen$, read as the
    squared residue $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$ through the named identification [H-Res] of Section~\ref{sec:levels}, carries
    the generation levels; and the mass comes after.

  \section{The determinant line \texorpdfstring{$\LY$}{L\_Y}}
    \label{sec:line}

    Two rank-two bundles must not be taken for one.
    The first is the spinor factor: under the spin solder [H-Spin] of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, the admissible doublet is the
    left-handed Weyl fibre of $\SW$, with structure group $\SL(2,\mathbb{C}) \simeq \Spin(3,1)$.
    The second is the weak factor $\Ew$ of [H-Weak], a Hermitian bundle of rank two with structure group $\UU(2)$,
    distinct
    from the soldered factor.
    By Schur's lemma the admissible doublet can supply at most one of the two~\cite{Beau2026q14}, and $\Ew$ is not
    constructed in~\cite{Beau2026q14} nor in any other paper cited here.
    The tensor functors are taken on a rank-two fibre, $\SW$ or $\Ew$, not on the doubled Dirac space
    $\Sbundle = S_{L} \oplus S_{R}$, so that no cross-chiral components are manufactured.

    \begin{proposition}[Projected Yukawa line]
      \label{prop:yukawa-line}
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item[\emph{(a)}] \emph{(algebra, proved on model data.)}
        Let $E$ be a rank-two bundle with fibre $\mathbb{C}^{2}$ and structure group $G \subset \GL(2,\mathbb{C})$.
        Among the functors $\Sym^{k}$ and $\wedge^{k}$ with $k \geq 1$, the determinant line $\wedge^{2}(E)$ is the only
        one of dimension one.
        (The powers $\det^{b}$ with $b \geq 2$ and the dual lines are also one-dimensional and lie outside this
        family.)
        If $G \subset \SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, then $\wedge^{2}(E)$ is a trivial line, and the adjoint sector
        $\Sym^{2}(E) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ does not see it.
        In particular, under [H-Spin], $\wedge^{2}(\SW)$ is trivial and carries no hypercharge.
        \item[\emph{(b)}] \emph{(under [H-Weak].)}
        For the rank-two bundle $\Ew$ with structure group $\UU(2)$, the line $\LY := \wedge^{2}(\Ew) = \det \Ew$ is
        acted on
        by $\UU(2)$ through the determinant character.
        It is trivial on the subgroup $\SU(2)$ whose algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}$ acts on $\Sym^{2}(\Ew)$ by the
        adjoint, and it carries the abelian weight of the central $\UU(1) \subset \UU(2)$.
        As a $\UU(2)$-bundle, $\Sym^{2}(\Ew) \simeq \mathfrak{sl}(\Ew) \otimes \LY$ is the weak adjoint twisted by
        $\LY$.
      \end{enumerate}
      Consequently, within the route of the functors $\Sym^{k}$ and $\wedge^{k}$ of a rank-two fibre, the line that
      carries an abelian weight is available only under [H-Weak], as a line of $\Ew$; the spinor factor supplies only
      the trivial line $\wedge^{2}(\SW)$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      (a) On the fibre $\mathbb{C}^{2}$ consider the symmetric and exterior powers $\Sym^{k}$ and $\wedge^{k}$,
      $k \geq 1$.
      Their dimensions are $\dim \Sym^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{2}) = k+1 \geq 2$ for $k \geq 1$,
      $\dim \wedge^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}) = 2$,
      $\dim \wedge^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2}) = 1$, and $\wedge^{k} = 0$ for $k \geq 3$, so $\wedge^{2}$ is the only
      one-dimensional instance of this family.
      The powers $\det^{b}$, $b \geq 2$, and the dual lines are one-dimensional too but are not of the form $\Sym^{k}$
      or $\wedge^{k}$, so uniqueness is a statement within the family and is not claimed beyond it.
      For $g \in \GL(2,\mathbb{C})$ the induced action on $\wedge^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})$ is multiplication by $\det g$.
      For $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ the symmetric square is the three-dimensional irreducible representation, the adjoint
      $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$, and $\det g = 1$, so $\wedge^{2}$ is the trivial representation, invisible to the
      adjoint sector.
      This holds for every subgroup $G \subset \SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, in particular for the structure group of $\SW$ under
      [H-Spin] and for any compact real form $\SU(2)$.
      The algebraic identities are those of Theorem~2.1(a) of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, which asserts the two identities and
      not the uniqueness.
      (b) The determinant $\det\colon \UU(2) \to \UU(1)$ is surjective with kernel $\SU(2)$, so $\LY = \det \Ew$ is
      trivial
      under $\SU(2)$.
      The central element $e^{i\theta}\,\mathbb{1}_{2}$ acts on $\LY$ by $\det = e^{2i\theta}$, so the abelian weight is
      non-trivial; at the Lie-algebra level a generator $\diag(y_{1}, y_{2})$ of $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ acts on $\LY$ by the
      trace $y_{1} + y_{2}$.
      That generator lies in $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ and not in $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$, so this weight exists only
      because
      the structure group of $\Ew$ is taken to be $\UU(2)$ rather than $\SU(2)$, which is part of
      [H-Weak]~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      For the diagonal element $\diag(a, b)$ of $\UU(2)$ the weights of $\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2})$ are
      $a^{2}, ab, b^{2}$, which are those of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2} \otimes \det$.
      The same typing of $\LY$ is Theorem~2.1(c) of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, under [H-Weak].
    \end{proof}

    The determinant line of the spinor factor is thus trivial, and, within that route, the line carrying the abelian
    weight is the determinant line of $\Ew$.
    Lines from other sources, such as the powers $\det^{b}$ or an independent $\UU(1)$ line, are outside the route and
    are not excluded.
    The symmetric square of $\Ew$ is the weak adjoint twisted by $\LY$, and the determinant line of $\Ew$ is $\LY$.
    [H-Weak] is a missing identification, not a refutation: the corpus does not supply the factor $\Ew$.
    The no-go note excludes two deposited candidates for an independent fermionic weak multiplicity
    $M_{L} \simeq \mathbb{C}^{2}$, conditionally on [H-Spin]~\cite{Beau2026nsr}; no other candidate is excluded
    there, and none is supplied.

  \section{Separation from the Yukawa operator}
    \label{sec:separation}

    Proposition~\ref{prop:yukawa-line} fixes a line, not a mass matrix.
    Under [H-Weak] the fermion fields are sections of
    $P_{L}\,\Sbundle \otimes \Ew \otimes \LY^{k} \oplus P_{R}\,\Sbundle \otimes
    \LY^{m}$ with integer exponents $k$, $m$ not fixed by the hypothesis~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    The line $\LY$ supplies the determinant line from which the weights of a coupling are built; the weight itself
    depends on $k$ and $m$, which [H-Weak] alone does not fix.
    Q14 selects them conditionally, under both hypotheses, a supplied colour module, anomaly constraints and a minimal
    normalisation of $\LY$ (Proposition~4.5 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).
    The mass matrix is a distinct object, a projected morphism of the admissible spinor sectors of the schematic form
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:yukawa-operator}
      Y_{\Pi}:\ P_{L}\,\Sbundle \otimes \Ew \otimes \LY^{k} \longrightarrow P_{R}\,\Sbundle \otimes \LY^{m},
    \end{equation}
    or equivalently its spectral avatar after compression by the projective residue $\Epi$.
    The form~\eqref{eq:yukawa-operator} is schematic, and its typing has one missing element and one ruled-out reading.
    The missing element is a weak linking carrier $K$, a finite-dimensional $\UU(2)$-module linking the weak doublet to
    the weak singlet, supplied by no source; without it an invariant coupling between the two sectors does not exist as
    typed.
    The Lorentz part is not the missing element: with the antilinear lift $\JPi$ of Theorem~3.7 of~\cite{Beau2026q14},
    which uses [H-Spin], the sesquilinear pairing $\overline{P_{L}\,\Sbundle} \otimes P_{R}\,\Sbundle$ contains the
    Lorentz scalar exactly once, as in Section~2 of~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
    The ruled-out reading is a linear Lorentz-equivariant map $P_{L}\,\Sbundle \to P_{R}\,\Sbundle$: under [H-Spin]
    the chiral factors $S_{L}$ and $S_{R} \simeq \overline{S_{L}}$ are inequivalent $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$-modules
    (Lemma~3.6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}), so a non-zero equivariant linear map does not exist, by Schur's lemma.
    Here $\JPi$ is the spinor-level lift exchanging the chiralities; it is not the internal parity of the generation
    copy that the companion note~\cite{Beau2026pyo} also writes $\JPi$, and Q14 supplies it.
    The factorisation premise [H-Fac] of the companion note~\cite{Beau2026pyo} reads the full morphism $Y_{\Pi}$
    through its generation block alone.
    It has two independent conditions: (F1) a weak linking carrier $K$, supplied by no source, for which the space
    $I_{K}$ of Lorentz-invariant sesquilinear couplings that are $\UU(2)$-invariant is non-zero; (F2) $\dim I_{K} \leq
    1$, so that the coupling factorises through $\Cgen$.
    It is not used here.
    The line $\LY$ is one ingredient of the abelian weight of the coupling; the operator $Y_{\Pi}$ fixes how strongly,
    and in which generation directions, the chiralities are tied.
    This separation is the central guardrail of the note: the line is not the operator, and the choice of the coupling
    line does not determine the operator.
    The norm of $Y_{\Pi}$, the orientation (sign) of the split coefficient $u$, and the mixing are downstream data,
    recorded as open in the sub-programme inventory~\cite{Beau2026fmnote}; none of them is supplied here.
    Q14 excludes a complex metaplectic phase as a source of $\Rmix$ within its model (Remark~6.4
    of~\cite{Beau2026q14}): the external block $\Rmix$ is not reached by the image of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$.
    That remark concerns $\Rmix$ only.
    The internal block $e_{0} \leftrightarrow e_{\pm}$ is treated in the companion note~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
    There $A_{\Pi}$, the anti-Hermitian part of the $\JPi$-odd part of the $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ lift of the step
    generator, vanishes identically for all complex coefficients, because with the antilinear $\JPi$ of Q14 the
    $\JPi$-odd part of the lift is its Hermitian part.
    This is a statement about $A_{\Pi}$ as defined.
    The $\JPi$-even anti-Hermitian part of the lift has the non-zero internal entry $\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(q -
    \bar{p})$, non-zero for real $p \neq q$, so the image of $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}$ does reach the internal block through
    that part.
    No physical exclusion of the internal block is claimed: whether $A_{\Pi}$, rather than the $\JPi$-even part, is the
    right object is a modelling choice of the companion note that no source justifies, and Q14 excludes the internal
    block only by hypothesis (Proposition~6.3(i)--(ii) of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).

  \section{Generation-level ordering}
    \label{sec:levels}

    The generation triplet is $\Cgen = \mathrm{span}(e_{0}, e_{+}, e_{-})$ with
    $\Cgen \simeq \Sym^{2}(V_{\mathrm{gen}})$
    for a distinct supplied doublet $V_{\mathrm{gen}}$, which is not the doublet soldered by
    [H-Spin]~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    The identification is supplied, not derived, and Q14 describes the transfer of the identification of O30 to this
    triplet as a further modelling step (Section~6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).
    The operators on $\Cgen$ of~\cite{Beau2026q14} are model operators, standing for the restriction of the squared
    projective residue $\Epi^{2}$, the zero-order spectral residue of non-injective projection in the spinorial
    sector~\cite{Beau2026prs}.
    The model operator is $(C_{2} - \Jthree^{2})/C_{2} + u\,\Jthree + v\,\Rmix$ with Cartan generator
    $\Jthree = \diag(0, 1, -1)$ and spin-one Casimir $C_{2} = 2$~\cite{Beau2026prs}.
    The CP-even real sector imposes $v = 0$.
    The value $(C_{2} - \Jthree^{2})/C_{2} = \diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ is algebraic at
    $C_{2} = 2$.
    The labelling $e_{\pm}$ ($\Jthree = \pm 1$) is the convention of Section~6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    The following hypothesis is used wherever the model operator is read as $\Epi^{2}$.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textbf{[H-Res]} (identification of the model operator; not supplied).
    The model operator $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ on $\Cgen = \Sym^{2}(V_{\mathrm{gen}})$, with $v = 0$, is
    the
    restriction of $\Epi^{2}$ to $\Cgen$.
    \medskip

    [H-Res] is a missing identification, not a refutation: Q14 treats the operators on $\Cgen$ as linear algebra and
    states
    that their reading as restrictions of $\Epi^{2}$ is not supplied (Section~6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).
    The Schur reduction of~\cite{Beau2026prs} states the same normal form on the supplied triplet, and it is read here
    as conditional on [H-Res].
    Its reading as the Born--Infeld even sector would require a further identification between the
    conditional $3\times3$ model of~\cite{Beau2026a34} on $\Sym^2(V_\rho)$ and
    $(E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}$; no such identification is available, so that reading is not used
    here.
    The algebra below concerns the model operator and does not depend on [H-Res]; the hypothesis enters only when the
    levels are called levels of $\Epi^{2}$.

    \begin{proposition}[Generation-level ordering]
      \label{prop:generation-level-assignment}
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item[\emph{(a)}] \emph{(algebra.)}
        This is algebra on the model operator, and no hypothesis on $\Epi^{2}$ is used.
        Let $s_{0} = 1$ be the level of $e_{0}$ for the model operator
        \[
          \diag\!\left(1,\ \tfrac12 + u,\ \tfrac12 - u\right)
        \]
        on $\Cgen$ with the weight basis $(e_{0}, e_{+}, e_{-})$, and define
        $d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{i}) := s_{0} - s_{i}$.
        Then
        \[
          d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{0}) = 0,
          \qquad
          d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+}) = \tfrac12 - u,
          \qquad
          d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{-}) = \tfrac12 + u,
        \]
        and $0 < d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+}) < d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{-})$ if and only if $0 < u < \tfrac12$.
        For $0 < u < \tfrac12$, $e_{0}$ has the smallest and $e_{-}$ the largest exit deficit.
        The ratio of the two outer levels is invariant under $s_{i} \mapsto \lambda\,s_{i}$ with $\lambda > 0$.
        \item[\emph{(b)}] \emph{(under [H-Res].)}
        The levels of the model operator are the levels of $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$, and the exit deficits of~(a) are the
        exit deficits of Definition~6.6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14} for that restriction.
      \end{enumerate}
      The ordering determines three ordered levels.
      It does not determine the map from levels to generations, which is not established~\cite{Beau2026q14}, nor the
      physical mass normalisation, which depends on the projected Yukawa norm and the upstream dictionary data.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      (a) The normal form of~\cite{Beau2026prs} with $v = 0$ is $(C_{2} - \Jthree^{2})/C_{2} + u\,\Jthree$, whose
      algebraic even part is $\diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ at $C_{2} = 2$; its eigenvalues on
      $(e_{0}, e_{+}, e_{-})$ are $(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$.
      The deficits are the differences with $s_{0} = 1$.
      The ordering follows from two inequalities: $d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+}) = \tfrac12 - u > 0$ if and only if
      $u < \tfrac12$, and $d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{-}) - d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+}) = 2u > 0$ if and only if $u > 0$.
      Together they place the central direction $e_{0}$ at zero deficit and $e_{-}$ at the largest deficit exactly when
      $0 < u < \tfrac12$.
      For $u > \tfrac12$ one has $d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+}) < 0$, so $e_{0}$ is not the smallest deficit.
      The outer levels are $s_{+} = d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{-})$ and $s_{-} = d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+})$, so their
      positivity follows from the ordering.
      The ratio $(\tfrac12 + u)/(\tfrac12 - u)$ is unchanged when both levels are multiplied by $\lambda$.
      (b) Under [H-Res] the model operator is $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$.
      The exit deficit of Q14 is $s_{0} - s_{i}$ for the eigenvalues of that restriction, with $s_{0}$ the level of the
      central direction, fixed by the internal parity of the generation copy; Q14 relates it to the support-exit
      distance from the non-exiting central level of O1~\cite{Beau2026a6}.
      Under [H-Res], $\Epi^{2} = \Epi^{\dagger}\Epi \succeq 0$ with $\Epi = -M^{\dagger}M$~\cite{Beau2026prs}, so the
      levels are non-negative.
      For $u = \tfrac12$ the level of $e_{-}$ is zero, and for $u > \tfrac12$ it is negative, which is excluded; hence
      $u \leq \tfrac12$, and the strict ordering needs $u < \tfrac12$.
      The lower side is symmetric: the level of $e_{+}$ is non-negative only for $u \geq -\tfrac12$.
      The ordering of~(a) is stated for $u > 0$, where $e_{-}$ has the largest deficit; for $u < 0$ the roles of $e_{+}$
      and $e_{-}$ exchange.
    \end{proof}

    The level structure is dimensionless and fixed by $u$ alone: the ratio $(\tfrac12 + u)/(\tfrac12 - u)$, positive
    for $u < \tfrac12$, is invariant under the spectral rescaling $\Epi^{2} \mapsto \lambda\,\Epi^{2}$
    of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, so the level ordering does not depend on the absolute spectral normalisation.
    The numerical value of $u$ is itself dictionary-bound through the chiral-frontier normalisation
    $\mathcal{N}_{A}$~\cite{Beau2026aar}, and is not fixed in this note; what is established here is the ordering of the
    three levels $e_{0}, e_{+}, e_{-}$, prior to any identification with the physical $e, \mu, \tau$.

  \section{Status of results}
    \label{sec:status}

    \paragraph{Proved (algebra on supplied model data).}
      On a rank-two bundle the determinant line $\wedge^{2}$ is the unique one-dimensional functor among the symmetric
      and exterior powers, and it is trivial when the structure group lies in $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, in which case
      $\Sym^{2}$ is the adjoint sector (Proposition~\ref{prop:yukawa-line}(a)).
      The exit deficits, the ordering for $0 < u < \tfrac12$ and the scale invariance of the level ratio of the
      model operator $\diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$ are algebraic, with no hypothesis on $\Epi^{2}$
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:generation-level-assignment}(a)).
      No step invokes the conditional model of~\cite{Beau2026a34}.

    \paragraph{Conditional.}
      Under [H-Spin], the line $\wedge^{2}(\SW)$ is trivial and carries no hypercharge.
      Under [H-Weak], $\LY = \wedge^{2}(\Ew)$ with structure group $\UU(2)$ carries the abelian weight
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:yukawa-line}(b)).
      Under [H-Res], the ordered levels are levels of $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:generation-level-assignment}(b)).
      The exponents $k$ and $m$ are selected by Q14 under both hypotheses, a supplied colour module, anomaly
      constraints and a minimal normalisation of $\LY$ (Proposition~4.5 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).
      Left-admissibility is an input of Q14, conditional on [H-Spin] and on the Born--Infeld datum (H1)--(H2) of O30,
      and is not used here beyond the separation from $V-A$.

    \paragraph{Ruled out (within the model of Q14).}
      A complex metaplectic phase as a source of the external block $\Rmix$, which the image of
      $\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$ does not reach (Remark~6.4 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).
      A linear Lorentz-equivariant map $P_{L}\,\Sbundle \to P_{R}\,\Sbundle$ under [H-Spin]
      (Lemma~3.6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).

    \paragraph{Open (missing elements).}
      The factor $\Ew$ and the spin solder [H-Spin], neither supplied by a source; the identification [H-Res]; the
      weak linking carrier $K$ linking the weak doublet to the weak singlets in~\eqref{eq:yukawa-operator}, the only
      missing element of the coupling, the Lorentz scalar being available; the exponents $k$ and $m$ beyond their
      conditional
      selection by Q14; the map from levels to generations; the projected Yukawa operator $Y_{\Pi}$, its norm, the
      orientation (sign) of $u$, and the mixing, including the internal block $e_{0} \leftrightarrow e_{\pm}$, which
      Remark~6.4 of Q14 does not cover and for which the question whether $A_{\Pi}$ is the right object is open in the
      companion note~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
      Hence the absolute masses, the mass ratios beyond the dimensionless level structure, and the generation mixing
      remain open, in agreement with the open deliverables of~\cite{Beau2026fmnote}.

    \paragraph{Summary.}
      The note records the algebraic content of the Yukawa line and of the level ordering together with the
      hypotheses that give them a physical reading, not the mass values.
      Under [H-Weak] the determinant line $\LY$ supplies the line from which the weights are built; under [H-Res] the
      model operator on $\Cgen$ carries the levels of $\Epi^{2}|_{\Cgen}$; the mass comes after.

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook (a reading, not a result).}
      The hypercharge line is where the Yukawa sector meets the weak interaction: the determinant line of the spinor
      factor is trivial, so within the tensor-functor route the line that couples to hypercharge must come from a second
      rank-two factor.
      The generation levels are a dimensionless pattern fixed by one number $u$; whether that pattern is a mass
      hierarchy depends on the missing identifications listed above.

  \section{Reproducibility}
    \label{sec:repro}

    The script \texttt{code/front3\_yukawa\_line.py} of this repository verifies the algebraic identities by exact
    symbolic computation (23 checks).
    For Proposition~\ref{prop:yukawa-line}, the script computes $\dim \Sym^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2}) = 3$,
    $\dim \wedge^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2}) = 1$ and $\dim \mathfrak{sl}_{2} = 3$ from the defining subspaces of
    $\mathbb{C}^{2} \otimes \mathbb{C}^{2}$, the action of $g \otimes g$ on $e_{1} \wedge e_{2}$, which equals $\det g$,
    its triviality on the $\SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ slice and on a general element of $\SU(2)$ and its non-triviality
    for $g = 2\,\mathbb{1}$ (check B), and the weight $y_{1} + y_{2}$ of a diagonal generator on that line (check C).
    The checks B and C are run separately and on matrices of $\mathbb{C}^{2}$ only: the script does not enumerate the
    functors $\Sym^{k}$ and $\wedge^{k}$ (the uniqueness of $\wedge^{2}$ within the family rests on the dimension count
    of the proof), and it does not construct $\Ew$ or test that a generator belongs to the structure group of
    $\Sbundle$ or of $\Ew$; that membership is an input, [H-Spin] for the spinor factor and [H-Weak] for the weak
    factor.
    For Proposition~\ref{prop:generation-level-assignment}, the script checks the normal form
    $(C_{2} - \Jthree^{2})/C_{2} + u\,\Jthree = \diag(1, \tfrac12 + u, \tfrac12 - u)$, its even-sector reduction to
    $\diag(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ at $u = 0$, the exit deficits read from the diagonal of that operator, and the
    scale invariance of the level ratio, an identity that is labelled as such.
    For the ordering it solves the system $0 < d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+}) < d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{-})$ and checks that
    its solution set is exactly $0 < u < \tfrac12$, that positivity of the two outer levels adds no condition, and
    that $e_{0}$ has the smallest deficit exactly for $|u| \leq \tfrac12$, together with an exact rational
    grid of $49$ values inside the interval and five values outside it
    ($u = -\tfrac1{10}$, $0$, $\tfrac12$, $\tfrac7{10}$, $1$);
    the identity $d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{-}) - d_{\mathrm{exit}}(e_{+}) = 2u$ is kept as a separate, labelled identity.
    These are checks on the model operator; the script does not test [H-Res], the reading of the model operator as the
    restriction of $\Epi^{2}$, nor the map from levels to generations.
    The directory \texttt{code/} also holds four earlier audit scripts of statements of the companion
    note~\cite{Beau2026pyo}, namely the polar class, its non-triviality, the transverse route and the reality
    structure of the step generator; none of them is used by a statement of this note.
    They do not reproduce the companion note: its own scripts, listed in its Reproducibility section, are held in the
    \texttt{code/} directory of the repository of that note.
